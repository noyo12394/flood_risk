FloodRisk FYRE — User Guide

A 3D flood-resilience game for a riverside campus. Make decisions, watch the flood rise, see what fails.

▶ Play: final-eight-theta-85.vercel.app

1. Open & basic controls

  1. Open the link above in a desktop browser (Chrome, Safari, Edge, Firefox).
  2. On the start screen, choose one of the three modes below.
  3. In any 3D view: drag = orbit · scroll / pinch = zoom · click a building = scan it (damage, flood depth, power, loss).
  4. To exit a mode, click ✕ (top-left) or the ≈ logo to return to the menu.
Reading the campus: lit windows = powered, dark windows = no power · cyan power lines = energized, red lines = failed · a red ⚡ OUT / ⚡ DARK tag over a building means it lost power (DARK = dry but still lost power).

2. Live Drill ⏱ TIMED + SCORE

A timed emergency. You begin at Day -5 and respond as a surprise storm develops.

  1. Prep: review the goals: protect people, keep lifelines online, and reduce damage.
  2. Click Start the drill. The clock begins at Day -5 and storm severity is revealed through the rising water.
  3. Answer each decision card. The clock pauses while you compare each option's cost, completion time, and expected effect. Open Read more for extra context.
  4. Watch the impact panel and 3D campus. Barriers, evacuation, pumps, and lifeline protection take time to finish. Long-term work may not help the current flood.
  5. Track the top bar: day, water level, damage, people at risk, power, and budget.
  6. At the end you get a resilience score (0–100) and grade, broken down by damage avoided, people protected, lifelines, and smart budget use. Unspent money earns no points when outcomes are poor.
  7. Review your action sequence, then use Re-run drill to test another strategy.
Goal: highest score = lowest loss, most people protected, power kept on, within budget.

3. Decision Lab ⚖ COMPARE A vs B

Two tabs at top-right: Guided dilemmas and Free compare.

Guided dilemmas

  1. Read the situation and the two plans (A and B).
  2. Click a plan chip to view that plan's campus in 3D; click the other to flip.
  3. Commit to a trade-off: click the plan you would choose for the stated objective.
  4. See both outcomes side-by-side. The model recommends a plan for that objective and explains what each option gains and gives up; your choice is not marked right or wrong.
  5. Next dilemma to continue, or Retry.

Free compare

  1. Set Plan A (left) and Plan B (right): a flood scenario + any mitigations.
  2. Click a panel header (view in 3D) to show that plan's campus.
  3. Read the scoreboard: damage, buildings dark, people, downtime, spend, cost of risk. The better value in each row is highlighted, with a recommended plan and one-line insight.

4. Insurance Desk $ PRICE RISK

Price the flood risk like an actuary and score the book. Data unlocks in 5 levels: 1 Inventory (flat rate) → 2 Hazard (PML + hazard-scaled) → 3 Vulnerability (EAL + actuarially fair) → 4 Affordability (caps + full score) → 5 Financial model (insured value, deductible, reinsurance).

  1. Click Unlock Level N → to reveal more data, then pick a pricing strategy (locked ones show the level they need).
  2. Adjust the levers: loading factor and flat rate early; insured value, deductible, and reinsurance when Level 5 opens the black box.
  3. Read the Portfolio scorecard (right): the 0–100 composite and its three parts — Coverage (premiums cover expected loss), Affordability (under the rate-on-line cap), Profitability (combined ratio < 100% = underwriting profit).
  4. Click any building for its breakdown. At Level 5, trace a 100-year loss across the owner, primary insurer, and reinsurer. Green ring = premium covers policy EAL; red = under-priced.

5. Sandbox ◇ FREE PLAY

  1. Open How to use for the built-in walkthrough.
  2. Pick a flood scenario (top-right).
  3. Drag Move controls to reposition the panel, or collapse it to clear the scene.
  4. Toggle mitigations — the 3D campus, CAT dashboard, and live impact summary update instantly.
  5. Switch role (Emergency Manager, Engineer, Budget Officer, Risk Analyst) to change what the dashboard emphasizes.
  6. Click any building to scan its details.
  7. Click Run before / after reflection to compare your plan against doing nothing.
Risk = Hazard × Exposure × Vulnerability
You can't change the hazard (the flood), but you can change exposure and vulnerability. Watch for the cascade: if the power substation floods, every building it feeds goes dark — even dry ones on high ground. Often, protecting the lifeline (raising the substation, lowering the water) does more for resilience than reducing flood damage to individual buildings.

Educational tool with simplified, illustrative data — not a research-grade model.
